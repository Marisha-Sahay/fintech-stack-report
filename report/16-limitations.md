## Limitations

**Disclosure is uneven.** Fintechs that recruit engineers through their blogs publish a great deal. Banks publish very little about their transactional systems. For JPMorgan Chase, Capital One, ING and BBVA, this research found no public source on how applications reach the database or on the ledger model. Several bank and Medium-hosted pages also refused automated reading, so a few relevant sources remain unread. "No public source found" in this report means exactly that. It is not evidence that a company does not use a technology.

**What gets published is biased.** Companies write about systems that worked and migrations that finished. Abandoned designs and quiet reversals rarely get a blog post, so the record over-represents success.

**Some of the strongest sources are old.** Several key sources date from 2012 to 2021. Their years are given in the text, and the per-company research notes record later evidence where it exists. A design described in 2017 may have changed since.

**Job listings describe intent.** A listing shows what a team works with or wants to hire for. It is a weaker signal than an engineering post about a running system, so listings are graded Medium and labeled where they carry a claim.

**Some authors have a stake.** A database team writing about its own database, or a vendor writing about a customer, has reasons to present it well. Those sources are flagged where they matter. Where two first-party sources disagree on a date or figure, the research notes record both.
